# Updated direction — supplied references

This direction supersedes the first cream/orange concept.

## Identity and imagery

- London Home Services Group is the primary brand, using the user-supplied skyline/swoosh logo.
- Powered by Mango is the endorsement; the supplied Mango logo appears in the enquiry and footer sections.
- Use navy, emerald green and a light green highlight derived from the primary logo.
- The hero uses the updated user-supplied image with London Home Services Group branding on the vehicle, uniform and boxes.

## Layout

Follow the supplied Maverick hero composition: full-width photograph, left-heavy navy gradient, oversized white/green heading, checked service list, two CTAs and a translucent reassurance card. The navbar follows the later reference: a simple white bar with logo, navigation, phone and email. Ratings are omitted until there are verified ratings for this business.

Follow the https://londonsafetychecks.co.uk/ lower-page layout: reassurance strip, service filter pills and three-column cards, audience selector with side navigation, benefit grid, commercial enquiry section, process, FAQs, customer types, enquiry form, London areas and footer. Copy describes this London pilot rather than reproducing the competitor's commercial claims.

## Confirmed product scope

London customers request cleaning and moving services only. Each main category has six proposed subservices. Gas, electrical and plumbing work are outside this site's offer. Mango's AI agent calls and helps arrange a service provider. This remains a marketing page with no marketplace, account or payment flow. See service-architecture.md for the proposed future page hierarchy.

## Still needed for launch

AI callback endpoint, approved service list, provider availability, actual covered postcodes and privacy/contact content. The user-supplied phone and email appear in the navbar and footer. The preview form does not send or store details. All detailed categories and geographic areas are visibly framed as pilot planning.

Production build and TypeScript checks pass. Earlier browser checks covered desktop/mobile layouts and interactions. Browser automation was unavailable during the cleaning-and-moving revision; that revision uses build, type and rendered HTML checks instead.
